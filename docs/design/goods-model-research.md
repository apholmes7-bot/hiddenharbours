# Goods handling research model

This is an **editor-only model**, not a playable handling cutover. It exercises the four standing goods tasks together: hand transfers, supported stacks, container data, and carrier capacity. Nothing references it from a runtime assembly, scene, prefab, save DTO, or Resources folder. Core contracts and production adapters still require architectural review before a later implementation. P2/P4/P5 motivate physical capacity and recoverable refusals.

The model follows the recommendations in the goods canon proposal (#1042): an 8 kg gross one-hand limit, 16 kg two-hand container limit, 12 kg catch limit, a 0.62 m one-hand length and 1.15 m two-hand length, refusal on ordinary overflow, and no hand-carry of the proposed stock stacks. These are reviewable defaults in `HandlingResearch.asset`; existing GameConfig, species balance and hand code remain authoritative in play. The additional width/height envelopes are proposed data rather than claims about existing rigs.

Each piece retains one id, measured envelope and mass. One catch, one container and one tool are the distinct sharing kinds; only different kinds may share the two hands. Two-hand loads reserve both slots without displacing either occupant. A successful put removes only that held item and selects the first free authored placement. Capacity requires all of opening fit, placement fit, count, occupied envelope volume and gross mass. Placements cannot overlap. Quarter-turn yaw is explicit; fish cannot be stood upright to defeat length limits. The conservative layouts deliberately do not solve arbitrary packing.

**Overflow decision:** a normal full or incompatible put leaves the exact item in hand. Floor/deck overflow is represented by a separate `SetDown` call requiring a verified dry support; failed support leaves the item held. No automatic spill, tipping, live-animal floor placement, scene support discovery or persistence is implemented. A production caller must supply a real support and species-compatible placement. Containers retain their children when moved; nesting and containment cycles are excluded rather than adding later-phase nesting code.

Stacks are assessed bottom first, with distinct identities, matching rim groups, footprint containment, total count including the base, total height, each lower container's supported weight, each container's own gross limit and required locating lids. Shells, contents and separately specified lid masses all count. Only the top may be accessed independently. A hand carry additionally requires every member's explicit permission, total mass and the complete stack envelope; otherwise it needs a compatible carrier. This is a support-chain assessment, not a stack interaction UI.

Carrier tests cover a pickup bed, low wharf cart, future tractor trailer and future forklift as supplied data. Each load remains one container/stack, with exact local bed bounds, zero unsupported elevation, nonoverlap, obstruction checks, an allowlist and aggregate payload. Optional load-centre and moment limits represent rated fork configurations. Tractor cargo belongs on its trailer. These are **capacity models only**: no vehicle controller, machine, lift animation, route, restraint or implicit hand transfer is added. The generic load model does not establish that a particular game vehicle has these dimensions or can lift the load.

## Research content

One entity per asset under `Data/GoodsModelResearch`, using distinct `container.research_*` ids to avoid replacing the existing deck-container presentation catalog. No sprite, material or rig reference is permitted. Item/profile strings describe the prototype vocabulary only; they do not create a second species catalog. Dimensions use integer millimetres, mass grams and usable capacity millilitres. Unit conversion constants are arithmetic, not tuning. No random source or wall clock participates.

The [manufacturer catalogue](https://www.saeplast.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Containing-Quality-2023.pdf), pages 2–3, supports these selected revisions. It cautions that availability varies by factory and dimensions have manufacturing tolerances; this is a representative equipment reference, not proof that a particular harbour uses these models.

The pail and low pan remain **game proposals from the earlier design**, with explicitly unverified local dimensions and masses. The pail uses a conservative inscribed rectangular packing envelope inside the proposed round cavity; it does not claim all circular space is rectangular. Its twenty small-shell placements retain the proposed legacy count ceiling. The pan has two short-fish placements and a three-total stack ceiling. Gross limits, support ratings, hand limits, profile acceptance and all layouts are proposed game data, not occupational lifting instructions or certified product ratings. No buoyancy, preservation or live-holding ability is inferred from a container name.

Schema tests reject bad ids, invalid inside/opening dimensions, impossible volume/count, overlapping or unreachable placements and contradictory lift/stack policies. Asset tests load all six separate container Defs plus the handling Def, verify the selected measured revisions, and reject art dependencies. Existing tests are unchanged: no previous rule or pixel expectation has been weakened.

Future work must establish exact-size persistence, rejection-safe production transactions, physical source/access checks, measured carrier supports and the full boat-to-counter route before enabling this model in play. Industrial handling remains later phase. Revisit unmeasured lids, local pan sizes and provisional payload/stack ratings with their owning roles; do not infer them from the test fixtures.
