# Real gear: an Atlantic inshore working reference

This is a starting inventory from general knowledge of Atlantic Canadian fishing, **not a surveyed local equipment list**. Dimensions are outside length × width × height in metres; usable space is smaller. Except for the named tub spot-check, dimensions and loads below are rough class estimates. I am unsure which crate, pan, bag and hoist sizes are usual at the particular harbour being depicted; the owner can correct those without changing the handling rule. Colour alone does not identify a tub's size or rating. Capacities are working design references, not lifting instructions or game balance already approved.

One measured check anchors the scale: a [Sæplast manufacturer sheet](https://asia.saeplast.com/static/extras/files/1456739542-hk_460-and-660_customer_161015_en.pdf) lists its 460-class tub at 1.23 × 1.02 × 0.57 m, 451 L and 40 kg empty; its 660-class is 1.22 × 1.02 × 0.74 m, 640 L and 51 kg empty. Other revisions differ. These are machine loads even before filling. The remaining figures are explicitly estimates rather than product specifications.

## What the game should take

- Keep honest dimensions, visible contents, handles and load surfaces; make small pails, hand pans and machine tubs visibly different scales.
- Let identical, compatible crates stack; let specified empties nest. Filled, nested containers cannot multiply storage space. A cart or machine moves the actual loaded container.
- Keep banding, cold/wet holding, drains, lids, manual loading and short shore runs as readable work. Atlantic vocabulary and ordinary worn gear carry the setting.
- Use volume/shape for fit and a simple lift refusal for heavy loads. Tune fictional hand limits in Defs; they are not occupational lifting guidance.

## What the game should leave

- Leave brand catalogues, regulated handling detail and regional gear certification out of the simulation. Confirm representative sizes before an art charter is implemented.
- Leave realistic live-animal distress, blood, infection, processing gore and detailed workplace injury out. An animal can be alive, dead or spoiled without graphic treatment.
- Leave loaded-container buoyancy and accidental overboard cargo to a later danger slice; uncertain equipment must not acquire a float ability by guesswork.
