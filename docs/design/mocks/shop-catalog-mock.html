Shop Talk & the Wares Book

A shop is not a screen you open. It is a person you talk to, who reaches under the counter and turns their stock book around for you — same paper, same ink, same hand as her own notebook, but it is theirs and it is not on your key.

DATA

How a thing gets onto the shelf

Today stock is a scene fact: the catalog reads whatever vendor components a designer stacked on a stall, so adding a boat to a yard means editing a scene. The tag inverts it — the listing says where it is sold, and no scene is touched.

StepOne asset

An offer Def — the layer that already exists — gains a Listed tag, a section, and the sellers who stock it.

StepSwept, never cached

Resources.LoadAll under Data/Resources/Catalog, sorted and null-filtered, resolved on every open. The book's own pattern.

StepQuoted as today

BuyLogic is untouched: price, owned, affordable. No new purchase economics are written.

StepSold as today

Confirm routes through the vendors' existing TryBuy(). Money, save writes and Core events do not move.

already exists

The offer Defs

ShipwrightOffer, GearOffer, PotOffer, InstrumentOffer, plus bait, supply and licence Defs. These are the Defs the brief means — they are already the for-sale layer, and the tag goes on them.

new, three assets

Lots, businesses, vehicles

Priced offers pointing at the thing by id — never a price bolted onto VehicleDef, which lives in another module and must not learn what money is. Businesses point at M2-41's building Def rather than restate it.

retires

The dark overlay

BuyScreen, plus the self-installing P-key dev driver on every vendor in every scene. Both headers already call themselves placeholders for this. The P key returns to the ledger.

Two safe pieces

Phase A — the book replaces the screen, over the existing scan. No data change, no migration. Playable, rulable, and it throws nothing away.

Phase B — the tag replaces the scan. Stock becomes content; scenes stop carrying it.

What this does not build

No new purchasable content, no property or business gameplay (that is M2-42), no dialogue tree, no phone app, and no instrument grants — ADR 0039's save-schema debt is not paid by listing one.

The sell screen is not in scope

It shares the same dark palette and will want the same treatment, but a sell is a different beat — the market moves under you — and deserves its own pass, not a rename of this one. Flagged so it is not silently inherited.

OWNER

Six rulings before anything is built

Recommendations are given so a nod is enough. R3 is the one that changes the mock.

R1 · the only question that changes code shape

How does a seller id find the thing that sells?

With stock as content, seller.macaulay must reach the component that owns the purchase. A field on the vendors; a registry; or vendors become stateless services.

Recommend: a SellerId field on the existing vendors. One field, the purchase flow does not move, and it can become a registry later without touching content.

R2

Does the conversation hold while the book is open, or end?

Holding costs one deferred-terminal row and buys the “come back if you change your mind” beat.

Recommend: hold. It is the Animal Crossing shape the brief is asking for. Ending is cheaper and loses it.

R3 · the load-bearing one

A second book — or a tab in hers?

The whole design rests on this. A stock tab in her notebook is reachable on N from the middle of the ocean, which is a menu with a paper texture.

Recommend: a second book. Ruling the other way is legitimate, but it collapses most of Plate Two.

R4

Is browsing itself earned?

Every readout in this game is gated behind owning the instrument. Is a price list a readout? Recorded because the keystone rule deserves to be asked, not assumed past.

Recommend: no. A seller showing you their wares is the oldest un-gated information there is, and gating it would make the ladder illegible — the exact thing this panel is for.

R5

Lots and businesses now, or held empty?

Recommend: define the sections now, author nothing. Append-only ids are cheap to reserve and expensive to renumber; an empty section simply shows no stub.

R6 · taste

The seller's verbs.

Buy her / Take it / Put her right per kind, or one plain Buy? Cheap either way.

Suggest the per-kind verbs. One switch, and most of the voice.
