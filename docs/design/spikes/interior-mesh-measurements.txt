B (FLEET) — CAN A LEVEL'S OWN HULL FACES BE FOUND FROM LAYOUT HEIGHTS ALONE?
   Rule under test, and it uses NOTHING the classifier already rejected (no
   material name, no normal direction, no face bias, no inset threshold):
     a hull face belongs to the HIGHEST level whose sole is at or below the
     face's own lowest point, among the levels whose outline contains its
     centroid in xy — i.e. everything STANDING ON a level comes off with it.
     (The first rule tried was 'the whole face lies between sole and ceiling'.
     A deckhouse WALL fails that — its top is the roof — so the wall stayed
     drawn and the room stayed invisible at 3.1% of its own pixels.)
   AMBIGUOUS = the face STRADDLES THE SOLE PLANE (bottom below, top above) — the deck
   plate itself, which belongs to the level and to the hull at once. That count is
   what decides whether this is derivable or needs the art director's hand.
