Fish · lobster + rock crab · shellfish · the clam hod · the catch kit · the field guide plate.

The catch review's drop, passes B to F (2026-10-27 and 10-28), over main's pass 2 kit
(`docs/art/rigs/catch-pass-2-kit/`): the rigs the review changed or added, the files they and the
harness depend on, every sidecar regenerated from them, and hashes for all of it. Its files replace
the pass 2 kit's of the same names. What this README does not restate, the pass 2 kit's README still
says: the recipe, the anims, poses, rests and motion, the wiring and the layering are pass 2's.

## Load order
Plain browser scripts, one global each, no build step. The catch rigs as pass 2's: `isoSolid` →
`fishIsoRig2`, `shellfishRig2` (no deps) → `crustaceanRig2`, `clamHodRig` (need IsoSolid) →
`catchKit2` (needs whichever catch rigs the fill uses). The plate then needs the notebook kit's
`dialogueBubbleRig` → `notebookRig3` (in `docs/art/rigs/notebook-kit/`, main's, not carried here; its
`isoSolid.js` is this one, byte for byte), and last `fieldGuidePlateRig`, which requires FishIso2,
Crustacean2, Shellfish2 and NotebookKit. The plate's sidecar gives the whole order, `loadOrder`. The
catch rigs bake as pass 2's do; the plate draws through a 2D context, as the notebook kit does.

Strict scale stays: 32 px = 1 m in the world, scale 1 the real animal. Ringless (ADR 0031).

## The plate's contract
One knowledge page per species with a def (10), on the notebook kit: the def's words on the ruled
leaf, the species on the squared leaf, in the kit's room for a plate that is not part of a section
(`NotebookKit.layout().plate`). The rig draws no species and no furniture of its own: the species
come from the catch rigs, the page, frame, corner ticks and caption from the kit.
- **The page scale.** A page is not the world. A plate draws its species at P page units to the
  metre: a multiple of 20 (`STEP`), so every bar is whole units, and at most 2000 (`P_MAX`), where
  the 160 px cell holds 8 cm.
- **One rig pixel per page unit.** The rig is asked for its drawing at scale P / 32 (m = 1) and
  nothing is resampled. The kit's integer scale s takes a unit to s × s px, so the s2 and s3 plates
  are the s1 plate scaled, pixel for pixel (`probeScale`).
- **The fit.** `fit()` takes the largest P whose drawing the rig's 160 × 96 cell does not clip and
  that sits `PAD` (3) units clear of the plate's frame. The cell is larger than the frame's inside
  (153 × 84 units at 31 × 20, 83 × 74 at 17 × 12), so the frame binds every fit, and a species has
  one P per page size: 31 × 20 cells at s1 (the 4× zoom), 17 × 12 at s2 (3×) and at s3 (2×).
- **The bar.** The longest of 1 m, 50, 25, 20, 10 and 5 cm that is no longer than the drawing, at
  least 6 units (`BAR_MIN`), set in the caption row a cell after its label, straight, in pencil. Its
  label is the caption, in place of the kit's default (ruling 4 of 10-28). The running head and the
  tabs are the Notebook's.
- **The words** are the def's alone: DisplayName; Flavor up to its first team note
  ("<WORD> NOTE:"); MinWeightKg–MaxWeightKg kg; Seasons, AllowedGear and DepthBands where the def
  carries them, by `Gear.cs`'s enum names. `blocks()` sets them as the kit's sections. The rig writes
  no word but the bar's label.
- **The turn.** When the words run onto the squared leaf the plate turns the page: the next spread
  carries the name and the plate (`render().pages[1]`). One plate turns: the clam's, at 17 × 12.
- **The probes**, `probeFit`, `probeWords(words)`, `probeScale` and `probeInk`, all pass; the sidecar
  records each one's claim and result.
