## 2. Running the checkers

```
cd character-v9.2-import-kit
node tools/run-all.cjs ../skinned-character-kit            # 10 checkers, a few minutes
node tools/run-all.cjs ../skinned-character-kit --bodies   # and check-bodies: 548 bodies on worker threads
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS.txt
```

Each checker writes `reports/<name>.txt` and `.json` and ends `OK — no problems.`. The generators (`export-builds`, `check-options`, `check-worldfit`, `check-shading`, `compare-rig7`) compare with the committed file; `--write` rewrites it. `check-bodies` runs `runChecks` on N worker threads (`--threads N`, default cores − 1); it is the only checker that needs `worker_threads`. As in 9.1, I ran these files in a browser engine with the same file helper as `_run.cjs`; your Node run is the confirmation. `check-shading` will again differ in the last digits of the key vectors on your side; you regenerate that file.

## 3. Hair: no scalp where the hair is

**The rule.** Each style has a hair region on the skull, in canonical head z (1.135 chin … 1.522 crown; the eye line is 1.31):

| Style | Front hairline F | Corners C | Temples T | Top of the ear E | Nape N | Material |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| crop | 1.425 (higher on small heads: the fringe line) | 1.36 | 1.425 | 1.333 | 1.245 | hair |
| mop | same | 1.36 | 1.425 | 1.333 | 1.225 | hair |
| bob, long | same | 1.34 | 1.425 | 1.333 | 1.20 | hair |
| bun, ponytail | same | 1.36 | 1.425 | 1.333 | 1.26 | hair |
| buzz | 1.425 | 1.425 | 1.425 | 1.333 | 1.30 | buzz |
| bald | — the horseshoe at the back, 1.30–1.425, as 9.1 drew it | | | | | buzz |

On the front plane the region starts at F, on the front corners at C, on the back and the back corners at N. On a side plane it depends on the ear (y = 0.016 m front edge, −0.032 m back edge): in front of the ear it starts at T, over the ear at E, behind the ear at N. E varies a little with the head (1.330–1.336); `CharacterIso9.hairline(build)` gives the resolved lines.

**The skin that is meant to show:** the face and the forehead below F and C, the temples in front of the ear below T, the ear, and the nape below N (the back of the skull under a short cut). Everything else of the skull is hair.

**How it is closed.**
* The skull is cut along these lines (a face that crosses a line is split in its own plane) and every piece inside the region takes the hair's material, so a gap in the shell shows hair. This is how the buzz always worked; it now covers over and behind the ears.
* Every shell band is cut at the skull's rings. 9.1's bob and long ran one quad from 1.20 to 1.425 across the 1.30 ring, and the skull poked through it at the back: the band you saw. Bun, ponytail and mop had the same chord across 1.30.
* Crop, mop, bun and ponytail have shell over the ear (from E) and behind it (down to N); bob and long already covered the ears.
* On small and wide heads the fringe starts above 1.425; the corners now run up to meet it (the 4 px at the front hairline).

**`tools/check-hair.cjs`** (`reports/hair.txt`): 0 px of skull skin in the hair region over 250,880 renders:
1. mesh: 4,200 head geometries (every age class, shape, weight and head × every style): no skull face painted skin inside the region.
2. idle f0, 16 facings (yaw 0 = behind): your 168 bodies × 8 styles, no hat; and 28 bodies (4 ages × 7 heads, shape and weight at their extremes) × 8 styles × 12 hats.
3. two bodies (adult heart s0 w+2, your worst; child wide s1 w−2) × 8 styles × 13 hats × 16 facings: walk f0–f7, the most-tipped frame of every clip that tips the head more than 5°, and the head turned to each look-at limit.

The report also gives the skin meant to show (most in one render: face 123 px on a bald head, temples 26, ears 18, nape 83) and the painted scalp that shows through a shell (at most 123 px: the buzz is all painted). The golden `hair` check runs the same classifier on any build (idle f0, f3, walk f2, f6, four look limits, 16 facings).

## 4. Blink

`BLINK` in the rig, `faceClips[0]` in every build file, `overlays.blink` in every sidecar. The game plays it on its own clock over any clip:

| Step | Eyes state | Time |
|---|---|---|
| before | the clip frame's state (open, wide, left, right) | — |
| 1 | `eyes.half` | 40 ms |
| 2 | `eyes.shut` | 80 ms |
| 3 | `eyes.half` | 40 ms |
| after | the clip frame's state again | — |

As a clip: 4 frames at 40 ms (half, shut, shut, half), not looping. **How often:** wait a random interval drawn uniformly from **2.4–6.0 s** between blinks; start each character at a random point in its first interval so a crowd never blinks together; 15 % of blinks are followed by a second one 120 ms later. **Leave it alone** when the frame's own eyes are `shut` (sleep keeps its closed eyes). Every eye shape has its own half and shut; the golden `face` check draws both at S on every build.

## 5. Look-at

**Controls: two bones and two face states.** No JavaScript at run time.

| Control | Range | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| `neck` bone | 0.4 of the turn | `neck.local = clip.neck.local × E(0.4 yaw, 0.4 pitch)` |
| `head` bone | 0.6 of the turn | `head.local = rock.head × clip.head.local × E(0.6 yaw, 0.6 pitch)` |
| turn yaw | −60 … +60° | + turns the face toward the figure's +x (its right) |
| turn pitch | −15 … +20° | + tips the face down (the rig's pitch: the top toward +y) |
| `eyes.left`, `eyes.right` | replaces `eyes.open` only | half, shut and wide stay as the clip has them |

`E(y, p) = Rz(−y) · Ry(0) · Rx(−p)` in the rig's frame (angles in degrees), post-multiplied onto the clip's local rotation, before the deck rock pre-multiplies the head. In Unity (pos x, z, y; quaternion −x, −z, −y, w) that is `bone.localRotation = clipLocal * Quaternion.AngleAxis(-y, up) * Quaternion.AngleAxis(-p, right)` with up and right the bone's own figure-frame z and x converted the same way.

**Aim** (per frame, after the clip poses the bones, figure frame, unrocked):
```
eye   = head.position + head.rotation * headMid          // headMid = [0, 0.02, 0.17] m
d     = inverse(chest.rotation) * (target - eye)          // the target in the chest frame
f     = inverse(chest.rotation) * (head.rotation * +y)    // where the clip has the head looking
need.yaw   = atan2(d.x, d.y) - atan2(f.x, f.y)            // wrapped to -180..180
need.pitch = -atan2(d.z, |d.xy|) + atan2(f.z, |f.xy|)
turn  = clamp(0.7 * need, limits)                         // headShare 0.7: the eyes lead
eyes  = need.yaw - turn.yaw >  8 ? eyes.right : < -8 ? eyes.left : eyes.open
```
`CharacterIso9.lookAt(solvedFrame, target, share)` is the same arithmetic. With share 1 the head's +y lands within 1.7° of targets 2 m away inside the limits (golden `look` check). Vertical gaze is the head pitch: the narrow and heavy-lidded eyes have one pupil row, so an up or down pupil would not read at 32 px/m.

**The gaze states.** `eyes.right` turns the right eye's pupil out one column and leaves the left eye's on its nose side, where the open eye already has it; `eyes.left` the mirror. At S both pupils end on the gaze side. In profile the eye toward the camera shows its pupil across both of its pixels and the eye turned away shows two whites.

**`tools/check-look.cjs`** (`reports/look.txt`): gaze on your 168 bodies × 3 eye shapes at 16 facings: every facing that shows two eyes moves the pupils at least 1 px both ways; where one eye shows in profile, left and right read apart (504 / 504). Head turn on every hat × every style on the Fisher and every garment with the hood, idle f0 and walk f2, 16 facings, the eight limits (yaw ±60, pitch −15 / +20, the four corners): no scalp in the hair region, no neck through the hood, no see-through at the neck, collar or hood (117 / 117). The hood's neck piece was changed for this: at a full turn 9.1's opened at the nape.

## 6. Mount and dismount

**The default saddle cannot do it.** Its seat is 0.94 m, and a seated pelvis sits 0.075 m above it. The tallest standing pelvis in the build space, on tiptoe, reaches about 0.72 m. No body can keep a foot on the ground with its pelvis on that seat, and no body sits astride it either: its pegs are out of reach from its seat (astride 39–80 mm short on the presets).

**The clip** (mountUp on t; the dismounts run it backward):

| t | |
|---|---|
| 0–0.30 | four steps in beside the machine, the swing foot first; the standing foot ends beside its own peg, the swing foot just behind it |
| 0.30–0.70 | the swing leg goes back and over the seat (in front of it on the bench) to the far peg |
| 0.64–0.90 | the body moves over and sits; the hands take the grips. The standing foot has not moved; it rises onto its toes (up to 40°) if the seat needs it |
| 0.90–1.00 | the seat carries the body; the standing foot comes up onto its peg |

The pelvis is held low enough that every foot reaches its target, so a planted foot is never pulled off the ground. The pins add `phase.swing`, `standFoot` (ground / rising / peg), `swingFoot`, `toeDeg`, `seatGap_m` and `carried`.

**The fitted saddle.** `CONTRACT.saddleFit` is the default saddle scaled to **0.40** about the pivot, with the idle spot beside it moved in to 0.60 m (`saddleOpts(0.40)`). The golden `contacts` check gates the six saddle clips there on every creator-size build: every limb within 5 mm, no seat gap, and the standing foot planted on the ground from the moment the swing foot leaves it until the seat carries the body. `travel` gates the mounts there too. At the default saddle both report. **World fit:** every creator body mounts at any scale in **0.30–0.49** and sits astride at 0.34–0.69, so **0.34–0.49 fits every creator body for the whole saddle family**. 50 of 125 children fit at 0.40 (children are reported, as for every world contract).
