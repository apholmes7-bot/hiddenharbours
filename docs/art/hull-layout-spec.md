# The hull layout: one rig per hull, with its room, its cut and its walk

`hidden-harbours/hull-geometry@1` · `hidden-harbours/hull-walk@1` · checker `tools/rig-recipes/hull-layout-check.mjs`

Written 2026-10-09 with the three recreation boats (the go-fast, the bowrider and the express cruiser), to
ADR 0048 §8. The ADR is the decision; this file is the contract each hull rig and its walk keep, and the checker
holds them to it. Where the two differ, the ADR wins and this file is corrected.

Every hull is one full mesh, outside and in, from its own rig: the hull, the room inside it, the door's leaf, and
the walk. Nothing else describes the boat: no separate cutaway rig, no cutaway sheets, no hand-written sidecar.

## 1. The two schemas

| schema id | where it lives | made by |
|---|---|---|
| `hidden-harbours/hull-geometry@1` | `byId(id).geometry()`, in the rig | the rig |
| `hidden-harbours/hull-walk@1` | `docs/art/rigs/gameplay/<rig stem>.<hull id>.walk.json`, one per hull | the rig's `walk()`, written to disk by `tools/rig-recipes/hull-walk.mjs` |

**Versions.** Adding an optional field keeps `@1`. Renaming or removing a field, changing a unit, or changing
what a field means makes it `@2`; the checker's `GEOMETRY` and the writer's `SCHEMA` constants move with it. The
checker fails a file whose schema id it does not know.

## 2. The tools: plain Node, no network

```
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-walk.mjs                  # derive every walk and compare it with its file; writes nothing
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-walk.mjs --write          # write the walks
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-walk.mjs --fit            # also print the fit to character rig 10.3, fixture by fixture
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-walk.mjs --hull bowrider  # one hull
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-layout-check.mjs          # check every hull; exit code 1 on any FAIL
node tools/rig-recipes/hull-layout-check.mjs --hull express_cruiser
```

- Node 16 or later. No packages, nothing to install, no network. The rigs load verbatim in Node's own V8 (`vm`).
- The checker writes nothing. It takes about ten seconds for the three boats.
- The hulls both scripts know are the `BOATS` rows in `hull-walk.mjs`, one `{ rig, sym, hull }` per hull. A new
  hull is one new row.

## 3. The frame, the units and the cell

- Metres, unless a name says otherwise (`px`, `deg`). Angles in degrees.
- The hull's frame: **+x starboard, +y bow, +z up**, the origin amidships at the keel bottom on the centreline.
- Polygons are counter-clockwise seen from above. Holes are counter-clockwise too, and may run outside their
  polygon (the reader subtracts them).
- The cell: `PX` pixels a metre (32 on these three), `W` × `H` pixels, `pivot` {x, y} the frame's origin on the
  picture, `defaultElev` the camera's elevation (40°). Facings `dir` 0 to 7 are N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW.
- The picture, as every rig's painter makes it: θ = dir · 45°, e = elevation; x' = x cos θ − y sin θ,
  y' = x sin θ + y cos θ; screen x = pivot.x + x' · PX, screen y = pivot.y − (y' sin e + z cos e) · PX; depth
  d = y' cos e − z sin e, nearer is smaller. A pixel is covered when its centre lies inside a triangle
  (barycentric ≥ −0.001); the face with the smallest d − db wins it.

## 4. The rig (ADR 0048 §8 A)

### 4.1 Its file, its global, `byId`

The rig is `docs/art/rigs/<stem>.js`. It defines one global: `GoFastIso` (`goFastIsoRig.js`), `BowriderIso`
(`bowriderIsoRig.js`), `CruiserIso` (`cruiserIsoRig.js`). `byId(id)` returns one hull; `hullIds` lists them. The
global also carries the default hull's members under the same names, so a reader may use either.

| hull id | rig | label | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| `go_fast` | `goFastIsoRig.js` / `GoFastIso` | Offshore 39 go-fast | cabin (a cuddy) |
| `bowrider` | `bowriderIsoRig.js` / `BowriderIso` | Bowrider 18 | open |
| `express_cruiser` | `cruiserIsoRig.js` / `CruiserIso` | Express cruiser 31 | cabin |

A hull's members:

| member | what it gives |
|---|---|
| `faceList(state, tower)` | the exterior: every face but the room's and the leaf's, in the given state (§4.4). `tower`: none on these three |
| `partFaces(id, state)` | one separable part's faces (§4.4) |
| `interiorFaces()` | the room (§4.5); `[]` on an open boat |
| `doorFaces(t)` | the door's leaf at t (§4.6); `[]` with no door |
| `geometry()` | `hidden-harbours/hull-geometry@1` (§4.7) |
| `matsFor(scheme)` | the paints for one scheme (§5) |
| `walk()` | every field of the walk sidecar but the writer's three (§6) |
| `fitCheck(tol)` | the room against the shell (§4.5) |
| `GAIN`, `BIAS`, `LN`, `BAYER` | the shade: the shade index is (lightness under `LN`) · GAIN + BIAS + the face's `b`; `BAYER` the 4 × 4 dither |
| `PX`, `W`, `H`, `pivot`, `defaultElev` | the cell (§3) |
| `schemeIds`, `defaultScheme` | the paint schemes (§5) |
| `DWL`, `cockpitZ`, `loa` | the waterline the rig floats at, the cockpit sole, the length overall |
| `render(dir, opts)`, `renderCut(dir, key, opts)` | the rig's own pictures, for review. The game does not read them |

### 4.2 A face

`{ v, mat, b, db, lv, ro, it, tag? }`

- `v`: the corners, `[[x, y, z], …]`, in the hull's frame, in order round the face.
- `mat`: a paint's name; `matsFor` names its ramp (§5).
- `b`: the face's shade bias, in ramp steps. At −1 or below the painter also lights the face from its back (the
  rig's interior rescue, which the bake keeps: `RigMeshExtractor`).
- `db`: a depth bias toward the camera, in metres. The bake carries it in UV0.z and the shader takes it off the
  clip depth only (the true depth stays).
- `lv`, `ro`: the face's level and role, as numbers (§4.3).
- `it`: `true` on the room's faces, `false` on every other.
- `tag`: on a separable part only (§4.4): 1 the wheel, 2 the drives, 3 the cruiser's hardtop.

### 4.3 Levels and roles

The same numbers on every hull, so a mask means the same thing on every boat.

| level `lv` | id | | role `ro` | id |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hull | 0 | | shell | 0 |
| main_deck | 1 | | wall | 1 |
| house | 2 | | roof | 2 |
| bridge | 3 | | lining | 3 |
| below | 4 | | part | 4 |
| rigging | 5 | | floor | 5 |
| foredeck | 6 | | fit | 6 |
| top | 7 | | tall | 7 |
| | | | trim | 8 |
| | | | rig | 9 |

`ghostRoles`: shell, wall, roof, lining, part, tall, trim. These are the roles that come back as ghost when their
level lifts; `floor` and `fit` never do.

### 4.4 State, and the parts that move

`faceList(state)` takes the rig's state object. A face's geometry depends on state only where a part moves; it
never depends on the scheme.

| hull | state keys (default) |
|---|---|
| go_fast | `hatch` 0..1, `steer` −1..1, `trim` 0..1 (0.3), `tilt` 0..1, `prop` (deg), `drive` true/false, `wheel` true/false |
| bowrider | `top` 0..1 (the bimini), `hatch` 0..1 (the sun pad), `steer`, `trim` (0.25), `tilt`, `prop`, `drive`, `wheel` |
| express_cruiser | `hatch` 0..1, `gate` 0..1 (the transom door), `sunroof` 0..1, `steer`, `trim` (0.25), `tilt`, `prop`, `drive`, `wheel`, `roof` true/false |

**The parts the game may move later**, each separable in the mesh:

| part | hull | how it separates | its tags |
|---|---|---|---|
| the wheel | all three | **face group `tag: 1`**; state `wheel: false` drops it; `partFaces('wheel')`. `geometry().parts` gives its hub, axis, rake and radius; it turns about the axis | the wheel's level, `fit` |
| the drives (sterndrive legs) | all three | **face group `tag: 2`**; state `drive: false` drops them; `partFaces('drive')`. They trim and tilt about the trim pins and steer about the gimbal's vertical axis | hull, `part` |
| the hardtop | express_cruiser | **face group `tag: 3`**; state `roof: false` drops it; `partFaces('roof')`. The roof and sunroof panel are `top`/`roof`, the rails, radome and whip `top`/`fit`; the arch legs stay (`top`/`tall`) | top |
| the door's leaf | go_fast, express_cruiser | **its own list**, `doorFaces(t)`; `partFaces('door', t)` | the door's level, `wall` |
| the engine hatch, the sun pad, the transom door, the sunroof, the bimini | per hull | **by state** (`hatch`, `gate`, `sunroof`, `top`) | `geometry().parts` names each |

### 4.5 The room: `interiorFaces()`

- One mesh, the same at every facing. Every face is turned to the side it is seen from: the engine culls back
  faces and draws the room front-facing only, for the level it shows (the shader's `lvl.z` test).
- Tagged `it: true`, `lv` the room's level (`below` on the go-fast and the cruiser), and its role: `floor` for
  the soles and berth tops, `lining` for the liner and the bulkheads, `fit` for the joinery, `tall` for the
  lockers and the head, `trim` for the cut caps.
- **Inside the shell.** `fitCheck(0.01)` measures every room vertex against the hull's skin (its half-breadth
  and its bottom at that station and height, the raked forefoot included), the lid over the room and the room's
  aft end, and counts any more than 0.01 m outside. A rig's walk carries the result as `qa.fitCheck`.
- The room's paints are the hull's scheme's, with the room's own appended (§5).

### 4.6 The door's leaf: `doorFaces(t)`

- `t` from 0 (shut) to 1 (open), or a state with `doorOpen`. Shut, the leaf closes the opening exactly.
- Tagged like the exterior: `lv` the door's level, `ro` `wall`, `it: false`. `faceList` never contains it.
- The walk's `house.door` describes it (§6.7). The rulings: the door is shut until it is opened; E opens and
  shuts it; pressing on moves the leaf through t.

### 4.7 `geometry()`

```
{ schema: 'hidden-harbours/hull-geometry@1', rig, exportSymbol, hull, units: 'metres', frame,
  ids: { level: number }, roles: { role: number }, ghostRoles: [role],
  dwlZ,                                   // the waterline the rig floats at: the walk's DWL
  levels: [ { id, deck, sole, ceil, kind: 'hard'|'open', soleZ, ceilingZ, ceiling: { kind, lid, z, of, note } } ],
  cutaway: { ghost: { key: [entry] }, gate: { key: [entry] }, shows: { key: level|null }, note },
  door,                                   // a cabin boat: the walk's house.door
  parts: [ { id, group: 'tag'|'state'|'doorFaces', tag?, state?, lv, ro, … } ],
  states: { key: 'range and meaning' } }
```

`ceil` is null exactly when the level is open (sky over it). `riggingClass` (the bowrider) says what her rigging
level holds.

### 4.8 The cut: `cutaway`

- A **key** is where the player stands: every walk area names one (`cut`).
- `ghost[key]` and `gate[key]` are lists in one vocabulary: a whole level (`'foredeck'`), or one role of a level
  (`'top:roof'`). The gate list is **complete** (ADR 0048 §6.4, the lift-set form): it names every level it lifts,
  never just a lid, and it lifts at least the whole levels the ghost list does. On these three, gate = ghost at
  every key.
- `shows[key]`: the level whose room shows while standing there, or null.

| hull | key | gate (= ghost) | shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| go_fast | `deck` | — | — |
| | `below` | foredeck | below |
| bowrider | `deck` | — | — |
| | `cockpit` | rigging | — |
| express_cruiser | `deck` | — | — |
| | `house` | top | — |
| | `below` | top, house, foredeck | below |

`renderCut(dir, key, opts)` draws the gate for review: every face the gate lifts is dropped; the shown level's
room is drawn, its front faces only, moved toward the camera by the hull's bounding diagonal, its own `db` kept on
top. That is what today's interior bake does (`BoatInteriorGeometryExtractor`). Everything else is painted as
`render` paints it.

## 5. Paints: how a scheme reaches them

- **The faces are the same in every scheme.** A face's `mat` is a name; the scheme chooses the ramp behind it.
- **A scheme is a paint table:** `matsFor(schemeId)` returns
  `{ scheme, MATS: { name: { ramp: ['#rrggbb', …], off, dith } }, ramps: [ramp], M: { name: { ramp: index, off } }, roomMats: [name] }`.
  `matsFor({ scheme: schemeId })` is the same table. `matsFor()` gives `defaultScheme`.
- `off` shifts a paint along its ramp, in steps; `dith` scales the `BAYER` dither between neighbouring steps.
- `schemeIds` lists the schemes; `defaultScheme` is the one the game paints when none is chosen.

| hull | schemes | default |
|---|---|---|
| go_fast | hornet, corsa, biscayne, blaze, venom | hornet |
| bowrider | summer-navy, harbour-white, cranberry, tar-black, champagne, seafoam, bottle-green, squall-grey | summer-navy |
| express_cruiser | express-navy, harbour-white, cranberry, tar-black, champagne, seafoam, bottle-green, squall-grey | express-navy |

- **The limits, in every scheme:** the hull's paints keep to 16 ramps or fewer (its palette slots), the room's to
  24 or fewer (`_RampMetaInterior[24]`). The checker counts both per scheme (A7).
- **The room follows the scheme.** Its paints (`roomMats`) are appended to the hull's: `lining` is the scheme's
  white, `cut` that white's top three steps; joinery, carpet, linen, stone, porcelain and the cavity's dark are
  the room's own. The berths' `quilt` takes a hull ramp by name: the accent (`acc`) on the cruiser, the seat vinyl
  (`uph`) on the go-fast, as 10-03 had it ("the go-fast's accent is the livery's loud vinyl"). The bowrider has no
  berths.
- **The leaf** is painted from the same table.

## 6. The walk (ADR 0048 §8 B)

### 6.1 Written by the rig, never by hand

- `byId(id).walk()` returns every field. The writer adds only what a rig cannot know about itself:
  `derivedFromRigSha256` (the sha256 of the rig's text with LF line ends, so a CRLF checkout hashes the same),
  `authoring`, and `qa.character` (§6.11). It never edits a number the rig wrote.
- The checker regenerates the file and compares byte for byte (B3). A hand edit fails; so does a rig edited
  without rewriting its walk (B2).
- Serialised as `JSON.stringify(walk, null, 1)` and a final LF; lengths to the millimetre.

### 6.2 The top level

| field | |
|---|---|
| `schema` | `hidden-harbours/hull-walk@1` |
| `rig`, `exportSymbol`, `hull` | the rig's file name, its global, the hull id |
| `label`, `kind` | the display name; `open`, `cabin` or `ship` (§7) |
| `derivedFromRigSha256`, `authoring` | the writer's (§6.1) |
| `units`, `frame` | `metres`; the frame (§3) |
| `loa_m` | the length overall, stem to the aft end of the platform or grate. `loh_m`, `beam_m` also given |
| `DWL` | the waterline above the keel bottom: the waterline the rig floats at (`dwlZ`). It must equal the flotation table's row ± 0.015 m; for a new hull this value becomes the row (§6.12) |
| `cockpitZ` | the cockpit sole (the open main deck's sole) |
| `cell` | the rig's cell: `W`, `H`, `pivot`, `pxPerM`, `defaultElev`, `order` |
| `tags` | `{ lv, ro, ghostRoles }`, the rig's own |
| `walk` | `{ radius, stepTol, rule, spawn, areas }` (§6.3) |
| `house` | a cabin boat's door, levels and decks (§6.7); null on an open boat |
| `anchors`, `interact`, `cleats`, `washboards`, `_excluded`, `rock`, `qa` | §6.6 to §6.11 |

### 6.3 The areas

`walk.radius` is the walker's radius (0.23 m on these three); `walk.stepTol` the largest step between heights
(0.34 m). `walk.rule` says it in words:

> A step is legal when the target point is inside an area polygon and outside its holes, and |area z − player z|
> < stepTol. Ramps interpolate z along their axis between a0 and a1, and hold z0 / z1 beyond them. `cut` is the
> cutaway key while standing there; `cutHi` / `cutLo` replace it above / below `zMid`. Polygons are
> player-centre: already inset by the radius, so the reader never insets them again.

Each area: `{ id, cut, poly, holes, z }` or `{ …, ramp: { axis: 'x'|'y', a0, a1, z0, z1 } }`, and optionally
`headroom` and `headroomMax` (the clear height over it, lowest and highest), `onlyWhen` (a state the area needs,
e.g. `{ hatch: 0 }`), `door` (the part an area runs through), `waitsOn` and `note`.

- A **sloped or crowned deck** is a set of pieces, each flat or a ramp, within 0.02 m of the surface
  (`qa.surfaceFit`): the foredecks are pieces `foredeck_<n><c|s<k>|p<k>>`.
- An area may be a thin strip: where a passage is barely wider than the walker, the centre's polygon is narrow
  (the bowrider's walk-through to the bow).

### 6.4 The climbs

A climb is an area with `kind: 'stair'` or `'ladder'`, a `ramp` from one level's height to the other's, `zMid`,
and `cutHi` / `cutLo` (the keys above and below `zMid`). On the cruiser, going below is a walk down the
companionway stair (`kind: 'stair'`, `house` above, `below` under 0.70 m), not a cut. A climb that is drawn but not
yet walkable is an `_excluded` entry with `of: 'climb'` and its `kind`.

### 6.5 Every standing surface

Every surface a figure stands, sits or passes through is either an area that fits the walker, or an `_excluded`
entry: `{ id, of: 'area'|'seat'|'berth'|'door'|'climb'|'cleat'|'washboards'|'note', reason, waitsOn?, z?, poly?,
at?, headroom?, kind? }`. The reason is a sentence with its numbers; `waitsOn` names the character clips it waits on
(§8). Every level of `geometry()` is covered by an area within 0.10 m of its sole, or by an `_excluded` area entry
at its sole.

### 6.6 `interact[]`: every point E acts on

`{ id, action, label, prompt, at, reach: { x, y, z }, area, … }`, one per point: the helm, the seats, the door, the
berths, the galley, the head, the lockers, the hatches, the swim ladder, the anchor.

- `at` is the thing; `reach` is where the player stands to use it, on the floor of `area`: inside its polygon or
  within 0.001 m of its outline, at its height ± 0.01 m.
- Where the stand point is on no walk area yet (a clip it waits on), `area` is null and `offWalk` names the
  `_excluded` entry it stands on.
- `seat`, `stand`, `state` (the state key it drives), `clearAt` (the t at which a door is clear) and `waitsOn` as
  they apply.
- Required: the helm, the door, and every berth's interact.

### 6.7 `house` (a cabin boat)

- `house.door`: `{ id, kind: 'slide'|'hinge', face, y, x0, x1, z0, z1, sillZ, leaf: 'doorFaces(t)', lv, ro,
  clear: { width, height }, opensTo, clearAt, note }`; a slide adds `travel`; a hinge adds `hinge` {x, y, z0, z1},
  `maxDeg`, `swings` and `keepClear` (the swept arc, as points in plan).
- `house.doors[]`: when a hull has more than one door. Not on these three.
- `house.levels`: the hard levels (`['below']` on the go-fast, `['house', 'below']` on the cruiser).
- `house.decks`: per level, `{ deck, kind, soleZ, ceilZ, ceilLowZ? }`, as `geometry()` has them.
- **A doorway with no leaf** says so: an entry in `house.doors[]` with `leaf: null`, `open: true` and a reason.
  The 90's skybridge doorway is one (ADR 0048 §6.4): it stays open, and under the gate the deck sees the shell's
  inner faces through it until the room's level shows. An open boat's walk-through (the bowrider's windscreen
  centre) is not a door: no leaf, never shut, and the walk runs through it.

### 6.8 `anchors`

`anchors.helm` {x, y, z} (the helm seat), and per hull its others: `wheel`, `pilot`, `seats[]`, `painter`,
`sternEyes`, `ladder`, `platform`, `grate`, `hinge`, `gate`, `companionway`, `engineHatch`, `anchorRoller`,
`sunroof`, `driveMounts`, and `berths[]`: `{ id, interact, kind, topZ, head, deckheadZ, poly }` (the mattress top,
which end the head lies at, the deckhead over it, its outline in plan).

### 6.9 Mooring: `cleats[]` and `washboards[]`

- `cleats[]`: `{ id, type: 'cleat'|'eye', pos: [x, y, z], note? }`, every point a line is made fast to. Eyes that
  are not for mooring (tow or trailer eyes) are `_excluded` with `of: 'cleat'`.
- `washboards[]`: `{ side, width_m, pinch_m?, outer_edge: [[x, y, z], …], note }`, the side decks a figure works
  along; or one `_excluded` entry with `of: 'washboards'` saying why there are none.

### 6.10 `rock`

`{ frames, rollA (deg), pitchA (deg), heaveA (px), period (s) }`, the rig's own `ROCK`.

### 6.11 `qa`

- `qa.fitCheck`: `{ tol, faces, vertices, outside, worst, worstAt }`, as the rig's `fitCheck(0.01)` gives it.
- `qa.surfaceFit`: the worst gap between a deck's pieces and the surface, against its tolerance.
- `qa.character`: the fit to character rig 10.3, written by the writer: the rig's file, revision and sha256s,
  the 30 builds, the method, and per fixture the clip, the world options, the numbers and `builds_ok` of 30; and
  `waitsOn`, every character clip the hull waits on. The writer refuses a character rig that is not 10.3.
- `qa.leakPx` is optional and not in these walks: the checker computes it (R2).

### 6.12 Flotation

`DWL` is the waterline the rig floats at, the same number as `geometry().dwlZ` and the rig's `DWL`. When the
flotation table (`FleetFlotationTableTests.cs`) has the hull's row, the two agree within 0.015 m. These three have
no row yet: their walks' DWL becomes their row at the intake (ADR 0048 §6.7).

## 7. What each kind of hull carries

## 8. The character clips a hull may wait on

A fixture that no clip of character rig 10.3 can do is not faked: it is an `_excluded` entry (or an interact)
with `waitsOn`. These are the names used, and `qa.character.waitsOn` lists a hull's:

| clip | for |
|---|---|
| `sit` | every passenger seat (10.3 has no sit clip) |
| `helm_idle.wheelOpts` | standing at the wheel: 10.3's helm_idle takes no wheel position |
| `duck`, `crawl` | low doorways, low soles, the mid-cabin |
| `stairDown`, `stairUp` | climbs of kind `stair` |
| `ladderOn` | boarding from the swim |
| `drive.tiltDeg` | a signed rim tilt on the drive clip (asked, not needed by these three now) |
